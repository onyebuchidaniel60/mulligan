## Why there are two sets of numbers

Step 2 of the brief asks for beat starts by summing the tape's `Sleep` values. Those sums
describe *wall-clock* time, and VHS does not preserve wall-clock time: it drops frames while
the terminal is idle, so the encoded video is shorter than the tape took to run. Summing the
old single-take tape gives 75.6 s of wall time for a 43.8 s video — placing audio there would
desync every beat.

The video is also now rendered as three segments (`tape-a`, `tape-b`, `tape-c`) and
concatenated, precisely so the beats hold long enough to read.

So placement uses the **measured video timeline**, obtained by sampling the encoded file at
10 fps and locating the screen clears and output bursts. That is measurement, not a guess.
Both sets are recorded below.

## Mux

```
ffmpeg -i demo/mulligan-90s.mp4 -i demo/narration-1.mp3 ... -i demo/narration-5.mp3 \
  -filter_complex "[1:a]adelay=0:all=1[n1];[2:a]adelay=6100:all=1[n2];\
[3:a]adelay=14400:all=1[n3];[4:a]adelay=46400:all=1[n4];[5:a]adelay=51800:all=1[n5];\
[n1][n2][n3][n4][n5]amix=inputs=5:duration=longest:normalize=0,apad[a]" \
  -map 0:v -map "[a]" -c:v copy -c:a aac -b:a 128k -ar 48000 -shortest \
  demo/mulligan-narrated.mp4
```

`apad` + `-shortest` keeps the video length exactly; the audio tail is padded with silence
to 61.48 s.
